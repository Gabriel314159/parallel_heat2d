# Raw measurements

Measured on Vanda (nodes of two Xeon Platinum 8452Y, 72 cores) on 3 and 4 November 2026, one node per run:

- `strong_scaling_mpi_1node.csv`: experiment 1, both serial references and 1 to 64 ranks.
- `strong_scaling_openmp.csv`: experiment 2, the references and 1 to 72 threads. Its 9 error rows are a first attempt that the launcher refused (a rank bound across both sockets); they are kept.
- `weak_scaling_1node.csv`: experiment 5, the references and 1, 4, 16 and 64 ranks.
- `hybrid_gpu.csv`: experiment 3 with the Poisson solve on one and on two A40 GPUs.
- `correctness_report.json`: experiment 0, 12 cases, no failed check.
- `sessions/<experiment>/<session_id>.json`: the resolved plan and environment of each runner call.

The runs on four nodes (experiments 1, 3, 4 and 5 from 128 ranks on) are not in yet. `git_commit` is `unknown` in the rows measured on CPU nodes (`CN-…`), where `git` was not found; judging by the session times, experiment 5 ran at 445dd60, and in experiment 2 the references and error rows at 445dd60 and the threaded rows at 2340aab.

[metrics_columns.csv](metrics_columns.csv) is a header-only format template. It must not be treated as experimental data.

Commit compact raw CSV files and associated resolved configurations/environment records when experiments are run. Each experiment of `experiments/` has its own file, named after its experiment file (for example `strong_scaling_mpi.csv`); `scripts/run_benchmarks.py` appends to it. Experiments 1 and 5 run in two jobs that may run at the same time, so their rows go to `<name>_1node.csv` and `<name>_4nodes.csv` (`--metrics-csv`); join them before the analysis. Keep every measured repetition, its success/failure status and the exact Git commit.

`session_id` names one invocation of the runner; its resolved plan and environment are in `outputs/<experiment>/<session_id>/session.json` (copies of the finished ones in `sessions/`). `phase` is `warmup` or `measured`: warmup rows are kept but left out of every aggregate. `status` is `ok` or `error`, and `error` holds the message of a failed run. `git_commit` ends in `-dirty` when tracked files differed from that commit, and in `-unchecked` when the node has no `git` and the runner only read `.git` (then the files were not compared). A row's `elapsed_s` is the maximum solver-loop time across ranks for a parallel run, or the serial solver-loop time for the serial reference.

`total_cores = mpi_ranks * omp_threads`; In rows with status `ok`, `nodes` and the observed thread count (`omp_threads`) come from the solver's `RESULT` line; error rows hold the requested values; record the placement in session metadata. `cores_per_rank` is the fewest physical cores any rank's threads may run on (0 if the system does not report it); a row with `cores_per_rank` below `omp_threads` had threads sharing cores. `binding` says how the threads were bound to physical cores: `cores` (every thread on a core of its own), `shared` (two threads on one core, for example its two hardware threads), `unpinned` (a thread free to run on several cores) or `unknown`. Only rows with `binding` `cores` are valid timings. `poisson_device` is where the CG loops ran (`cpu`; in the GPU build `gpu`, or `host (no GPU found)`), and `poisson_copy_s` the time of the copies between host and GPU (0 on the CPU). `poisson_s` is the time of the Poisson solves in the timed loop; `poisson_halo_s` and `poisson_sum_s` are the parts of it spent inside the CG's halo exchanges (under overlap, only posting and waiting) and global sums. All three are maxima over the ranks, so they do not add up. `max_abs_error` is optional for performance-only runs and should be blank when not checked. `mass_error` is the change of the total scalar relative to its initial absolute amount, $\lvert M_{\text{end}}-M_0\rvert / (\Delta x\,\Delta y\sum\lvert c^0_{i,j}\rvert)$, computed outside the timed loop. For non-negative fields this is $\lvert M_{\text{end}}-M_0\rvert/M_0$; unlike that ratio, it is also defined for `cosine_decay`, where $M_0 = 0$. `peclet` and `peclet_grid` are $\mathrm{Pe}$ and $\mathrm{Pe}_{\text{grid}}$ as defined in `docs/design.md`. Analytical discretization errors need a separate validation record.

Do not commit full-resolution field dumps, build directories or temporary log bundles here. Large temporary files belong in ignored output directories or the appropriate HPC filesystem.
